## Post body

Kansas has 419,777 drilled oil and gas wells on the state's master list.

23,897 of them (5.7%) have a publicly-accessible digitized log.

The other 94.3% are dark.

That's not a scanning backlog. Look at the vintage curve:

𝟭. Pre-2000 Kansas averages 2% LAS coverage across every decade back to 1920.
𝟮. 2000s: 14%. 2010s: 38%. First decade where digital was the expectation.
𝟯. The east/west divide is a time divide. Johnson County (1920s oil patch): 0.2% lit. Wichita County (2010s horizontals): 43% lit.

Dark data isn't a mistake waiting to be corrected. It's a stratigraphy of time: most of the state's drilling happened before anyone was legally obligated to file a machine-readable log. The paper it was recorded on moved through M&A, warehouse fires, and retirements.

Every dark well is pre-drill intelligence that nobody extracted.

Kansas is the cleanest public data pipeline of any US state: a two-file download. Most of the country is worse.
